## Detailed Study Notes

### FortiGate and FortiOS

FortiGate is a network security platform. FortiOS provides its operating system and security features.

Understand the difference between the management interface, network interfaces, routing functions, and security policy processing.

### Firewall Policies

Firewall policies determine whether traffic matching configured conditions is allowed or denied. Learn the role of incoming and outgoing interfaces, source and destination addresses, services, schedules, and policy order.

A policy must match the traffic and be consistent with the intended network design for communication to succeed.

### Network Connectivity

Review IP addresses, subnet masks, default gateways, routes, and interface status. When connectivity fails, verify the network path rather than assuming the firewall policy is the only possible cause.

### Security Profiles

Study the purpose of common FortiGate security capabilities, including antivirus, web filtering, application control, and intrusion prevention. Understand that availability and behavior can depend on configuration, licensing, and FortiOS version.

### Monitoring and Logs

Learn how administrators use logs, dashboards, and status information to understand traffic and identify operational problems. Logs provide evidence for investigation, but must be interpreted in context.

### Administration and Access

Understand administrative accounts, permissions, secure management access, and why least privilege matters. Use authorized accounts and follow organizational security procedures.

## Practical Examples / Labs

Use a personal lab, authorized FortiGate environment, or Fortinet-provided training resources.

- Explore the FortiGate dashboard and interface status.
- Review a sample firewall policy and identify its matching conditions.
- Build a simple test topology and verify permitted connectivity.
- Inspect available logs for authorized test traffic.
- Compare expected and observed behavior after a controlled policy change.
- Practise diagnosing an incorrect IP address, route, or policy in a lab.

Do not change production systems or test networks without permission.

- Ignoring policy order and traffic direction.
